## Sequence, goals, prerequisites, and assessment

This is an advanced graduate research seminar organized around pre-trained language models. The schedule progresses through BERT, T5, GPT-3, prompting and parameter-efficient adaptation, calibration, reasoning, knowledge, data and scaling, privacy, bias/toxicity and mitigation, sparse and retrieval-based models, human-feedback training, code models, multimodal LMs, and interpretability. Students read papers in depth, answer pre-lecture questions, present research topics, review one another’s presentations, and complete a research project.

The goals are both technical and research-oriented: understand current LLM capabilities and limits, practice literature review and oral presentation, give constructive feedback, and conduct an empirical project. Prerequisites are prior machine learning and NLP courses plus familiarity with deep learning models such as Transformers.

Assessment is class participation 25%, student presentations 30%, lecture feedback 5%, and a final project/final paper 40%. The project may involve training or fine-tuning a medium-sized model, or prompting and evaluating a very large model. The official page also specifies a proposal, in-class presentation, and final paper deadlines for the Fall 2022 term.
